## Key Provision — 25 kW Threshold

The August 2026 amendment introduces a specific provision for distributed-generation systems with a capacity of **25 kW or less**.

For distributed-generation systems within this threshold, the applicable approval/concurrence process is treated differently from larger systems under the prosumer framework.

The concerned distribution licensee is responsible for the applicable approval process for systems falling within this threshold.

### Important interpretation

The 25 kW threshold is a **capacity threshold within the prosumer/distributed-generation regulatory framework**.

It should not automatically be interpreted as a requirement, exemption, or connection rule for utility-scale renewable-energy generation projects.

A project assessment must first determine:

1. The project technology.
2. The project capacity.
3. Whether the project is distributed generation/prosumer or another type of generation facility.
4. The intended grid connection arrangement.
5. Which NEPRA regulations and technical standards apply.

---

## Applicability

This amendment is particularly relevant when assessing:

- Residential distributed-generation systems.
- Small commercial solar installations.
- Small distributed-generation projects.
- Prosumer projects.
- Projects operating under the applicable distributed-generation framework.

For a utility-scale solar PV project, the Regulatory Intelligence Agent should **not use the 25 kW provision as the primary regulatory framework**.

Instead, the agent should identify the project's scale and connection arrangement and retrieve the applicable generation, interconnection, grid-code and other regulatory requirements.

## Evidence Classification

**Primary evidence:** The 25 kW capacity threshold and the associated regulatory treatment are derived from the NEPRA amendment identified above.

**AI interpretation:** Determining whether a particular SolarGrid AI project falls within the prosumer/distributed-generation framework requires consideration of the project's technology, capacity and connection arrangement.

**Project-specific determination:** SolarGrid AI should not declare regulatory applicability solely from project capacity. The applicable regulatory framework must be established from the project's actual characteristics and intended connection arrangement.

---

## Regulatory Significance for SolarGrid AI

The amendment allows the Regulatory Intelligence Agent to distinguish between:

- Small distributed-generation / prosumer projects.
- Larger renewable-energy generation projects.
- Utility-scale solar PV projects.

This distinction is important because a regulation designed for small distributed-generation systems should not be incorrectly applied to a utility-scale project.

For example, if a SolarGrid AI project has a capacity of 50 MW, the agent should flag the 25 kW prosumer threshold as **not being the primary regulatory framework for that project** and retrieve the relevant generation and grid-connection regulations instead.

---

## Limitations

This source does not by itself establish:

- The available grid capacity at a particular location.
- The appropriate point of interconnection.
- The technical feasibility of connecting a project.
- Project CAPEX or OPEX.
- Project revenue.
- Project financial viability.
- Site-specific permitting requirements.

Those questions require additional evidence and/or project-specific analysis.
